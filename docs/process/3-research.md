# 3. Research: go broad first

Goal: a pool of interesting, sourced material at least **three times** bigger than the tour
can use, gathered across many categories, so the topic stage (4-topics.md) has real choices.
The Hobart trial went deep on local history and too narrow elsewhere. Wildlife, the national
picture and music were the gaps.

Notes go in `tours/<slug>/source/research.md`. It is gitignored because it may name the family.

## Sweep every category, for the region and for each candidate stop

| Category | Look for |
| --- | --- |
| **Stories from history** | Moments with a person, a problem and a turn: disasters, escapes, inventions, feuds, firsts, failures. Convict, colonial, migrant and industrial history. |
| **First Nations history and culture** | Traditional owners, places and names. Use sources the community endorses, follow local spelling conventions, and don't invent or "voice" cultural knowledge. Note what is respectful to share. |
| **Wildlife you will likely see** | Seen from the road or at the stops: mammals (including roadkill hotspots, sadly common in some regions, and why dusk is risky), birds you can spot and hear, river life, insects, plants. Season, time of day, where to look. What it is, how to recognise it, one amazing fact. |
| **The national and state picture** | Primary industries (what's grown, mined, farmed, logged or fished here, and where it goes), the economy, major current news, controversies and debates (give the main sides fairly), politics only as context, sport, famous people. |
| **Landscape and science** | Geology, glaciers, rivers, climate, weather, hydro schemes, forests, fire. |
| **Place names** | Where they come from, renamings, odd ones. |
| **Food and produce** | What's grown or made here, and what can be bought or tried on the way. |
| **Music** | Artists from the region; songs about it or set there; songs tied to its history or industries; national music landmarks; songs on the stop's theme (rivers, trains, forests, the sea). Candidates for song segments (6-sound.md). |
| **Quirks and records** | The biggest, oldest, first, only and strangest. |
| **Possible guests** | Who could be "interviewed" at each stop (5-scripts.md): a scientist role, a historical figure whose own words survive, an animal. |
| **Sound** | Characteristic sounds of the place (birds, rivers, machinery, trains) as candidate SFX. |

## Rules

- **Record a source for every fact** (URL, publisher, date). Prefer primary and official
  sources: museums, government, parks, peer-reviewed work, reputable news.
- **Mark confidence.** `confirmed` means two independent sources or one authoritative source.
  `single source` and `disputed` are the other levels. Only confirmed facts become key claims
  in a script.
- **Get numbers right**, along with their units and dates. Scripts spell them out later.
- **Check currency.** For news and controversies, note the date and check it is still current
  just before rendering.
- **Get pronunciations** for every unusual name: the guide, its source, and whether it has been
  confirmed (this feeds `## Pronunciation` in the draft).
- **For songs**, record the title, artist, year, the link to the place, one great story about
  the song, and a verified Spotify link (6-sound.md).
- **Look past Wikipedia.** It's fine as a map, but follow its citations.

## Output shape

Group the notes under each candidate stop, plus a **Region-wide** section for material that
could go anywhere (national context, songs, wildlife that turns up all along the route).
Each item is one line, a fact, a source and a confidence level, plus a one-line hook: why a
child or an adult would care.
