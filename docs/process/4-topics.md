# 4. Topics: downselect the research into stops

Goal: a per-stop plan (anchor story, side facts, song, guest, SFX and title) that balances
the whole tour.

## Per stop

- **One anchor story** with an arc: a person or creature, a problem, a turn, and an outcome. It
  fits within the stop's time budget (2-stops.md).
- **One or two side facts** from other categories, ideally something the listener can see or
  hear right then: wildlife to watch for, the industry in the fields outside, a national
  connection ("the salmon farmed here end up on plates across the country, and that is
  controversial, because...").
- **Optionally, a song segment** (30–60 s more, task 006) or a **guest moment** (task 005).
- **A working chapter title**, "The <noun> That/With <surprising thing>", which teases the
  story without giving it away.

## Balance across the tour

Lay the plan out as a table (stop × category) in `plan.md` and check it:

- Variety: no two anchor stories in a row from the same category (for example two convict
  stories).
- At least 2 stops include **wildlife** you are likely to see on that stretch, and at least one
  invites the children to spot or count something.
- At least 2 stops connect to the **national or state picture** (industry, news, controversy).
  Give the main sides fairly, and keep it suitable for the youngest listener.
- **2–4 songs** spread over the tour. Not on back-to-back stops, and not on a stop that is
  followed by a short gap.
- **1–3 guest moments** in the tour, not every stop. Ideally a mix: an expert, a figure from
  history (only words they actually said or wrote), and an animal.
- **2–4 SFX per stop** where they add something (6-sound.md). The chime still opens every stop.
- First Nations history is present and handled with care (3-research.md).
- Give the arc of the day a shape: something gripping first, the big story at the parked stop,
  a lighter or reflective close on the way home.

## Pick, then cut

When there's too much (there should be), keep material that is visible from the road, that
surprises an adult, that a child could retell at dinner, or that sets up somewhere the family
will actually stop. Cut material that needs a map or a diagram to follow. Leftovers stay in the
research notes for a future tour on the same roads.
